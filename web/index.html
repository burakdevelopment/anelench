How it works

Measurements, not pixels

The Event Horizon Telescope samples the sky's Fourier transform at a few hundred points. Station gains corrupt each visibility, so Anelench fits only gain-invariant closure phases and log closure amplitudes, with a differentiable forward model of the real 2017 sampling.

Counterexamples on purpose

Image models with different built-in assumptions (entropy, total variation, two neural fields, a deep image prior, and two generative models trained on simulated black holes with and without knots) are each pushed to remove, then to strengthen, the marked feature while the fit stays within the noise a model would absorb anyway. If both sides survive, the feature is not established.

Plan for the hardest pair

For every feasible follow-up night, Anelench predicts what each witness pair would make the array measure and ranks packages by the worst-case separation per unit cost. The recommendation is then checked the honest way: by adding the new data and searching again.

Data: EHT Collaboration 2019, release 2019-D01-01. Related work: Feng, Bouman & Freeman 2024 (learned priors for M87*); Dahale et al. 2025 (ring ellipticity and spin); eht-imaging (Chael et al.). The design criterion is an approximation over a finite pool of explanations, not a guarantee over all images.
